01 Network reliability · AI 2026 – present In development

NeuroNet: An Early-Warning System for Home Internet

Can a home network warn its internet provider before it fails, so the customer never notices?

NeuroNet keeps a live digital twin of each home network. It reads the health signals a router already reports (line signal, errors, router temperature and memory, Wi-Fi interference), learns what is normal for each home, and flags when a home starts drifting toward failure. It then points to the likely cause (the Wi-Fi inside the home, the router, or the cable line) and alerts the provider to fix it early, remotely or with the right technician visit. A home-network pilot is planned.

Why this, and what it adds +
Origin

At Nile Secure I tested enterprise Wi-Fi access points across authentication, interoperability, and long-duration stability. I learned that networks rarely fail suddenly; they show small warning signs hours or days before. NeuroNet is built to catch those signs.

Where it's going

Stage 1 is early warning. Next comes automatic fixes for simple problems, such as restarting a stuck router or moving Wi-Fi to a quieter channel, then a self-healing network that looks after itself.

03 Model behavior 2025 – present Draft report · Co-author

Verbalized vs. Behavioral Eval-Awareness in Frontier Reasoning Models

When a model notices it's being evaluated, does its behavior actually change?

If models behave differently during evaluations than in real use, benchmark scores can mislead the people who rely on them. This study tests whether evaluation context, given through explicit system-prompt framing or labels already inside benchmark prompts, shifts how reasoning models respond, and whether the model's own "this looks like a test" reasoning explains the shift.

32,170pre-registered rollouts across frontier and open-weight models
+7.5–13.0 pphigher refusal predicted by benchmark eval-labels
5 of 6models significant after Holm-Bonferroni correction
Results, methods & limits +
What we found
  • Eval-labels already embedded in benchmark prompts predicted higher refusal in 5 of 6 models.
  • A previously reported verbalized eval-awareness mediation effect did not replicate in a pre-registered test.
How

Three experimental phases, two fully pre-registered, across models from several labs. We measured refusal shifts and checked whether verbalized eval-awareness in the reasoning trace explained them.

Scope of the evidence

The eval-label result is predictive, not a controlled intervention, so it shows association rather than cause. The report is still a draft.
